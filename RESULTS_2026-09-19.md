## Stage 8.6 — evaluation matrix

| image_set | CNN | CLIP | Ensemble | Held-out (ResNet18) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| original | 0.767 | 0.850 | 0.767 | 0.733 |
| cnn_only_hardened | 0.000 | 0.683 | 0.000 | 0.267 |
| dual_hardened | 0.000 | 0.600 | 0.000 | 0.350 |

Held-out attacker's own validation accuracy: 0.861 (macro-F1 0.771).

### What this actually shows

1. **Novelty claim supported on the CLIP column**: dual hardening beats
   CNN-only hardening against CLIP specifically (0.600 vs 0.683) — this
   is the point of the CLIP loss term, and it worked.
2. **Ensemble column is uninformative**: identical 0.000 for both
   variants. The 50/50 CNN+CLIP confidence average lets the CNN's total
   collapse dominate the vote, masking the CLIP-specific difference
   visible in column 2. The ensemble design needs rethinking (e.g.
   weight CLIP more, or require both to agree) before it's useful.
3. **Held-out result cuts against the clean story**: dual hardening
   (0.350) transferred *worse* to the untrained ResNet18 than CNN-only
   hardening (0.267) — the opposite of what "dual defense generalizes
   better" predicts. Both reduce solve rate substantially from baseline
   (0.733), so hardening does transfer some effect either way, but not
   in the direction Stage 8.4's novelty claim would want. This needs to
   go into the write-up honestly, not be smoothed over.
